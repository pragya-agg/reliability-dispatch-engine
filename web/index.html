What is measured and what is simulated

  • Fix a software bug: AI agents are 8 real SWE-bench Verified agents. Each request is matched to a recorded task the model never trained on; caliber is the model's prediction, and the result is the agent's recorded result. Human developers are simulated.
  • Parcel pickup: couriers are 8 real LaDe-P couriers; caliber = 1 − their average predicted late risk on a real day. Whether this pickup is on time is simulated from it. Delivery robots are simulated.
  • Everything else, plus costs, capacities and work times, is simulated from the assumptions below.

How it works

  1. Train. An XGBoost reliability model learns each agent's chance of resolving a task from .
  2. Replay. The newest SWE-bench Verified tasks, which the model never saw, arrive one by one in their real order.
  3. Caliber. For every waiting job the dispatcher scores every agent. The learning dispatcher also updates each agent's caliber per repository as results come in.
  4. Dispatch. Every round, waiting jobs are matched to free slots by optimal assignment (maximum total caliber).
  5. Outcome. When the work time ends, the agent's recorded result on that task decides success.

Real: tasks, task order, every agent's outcome on every task. Assumed: arrival timing, work time (from the SWE-bench difficulty label, an estimated human fix time) and parallel-job limits.

Limitations

  • Gains from model-based dispatch are real but small: about 1 to 2.5 points over the best-track-record rule, far below the oracle.
  • Online learning during the replay did not add a measurable gain over the trained model.
  • Couriers are monitored, not re-dispatched: the outcome of giving a job to a different courier is not recorded.
  • Humans and AI agents come from separate datasets; routing one job between a human and an agent is not tested. Robots are out of scope.
